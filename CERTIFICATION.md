# Certification & Integrity Requirements

## United States (USDA National Organic Program)

- Any operation that **handles** organic agricultural products (processing, packaging, combining, etc.) generally requires certification unless it qualifies for a specific retail exemption.
- A **central commissary** that cooks, mixes, packages, or otherwise processes organic ingredients must be certified as an organic handler/processor.
- Retail spokes that only perform final assembly and heating at the point of sale may qualify for certain exemptions, but claiming “USDA Certified Organic” on multi-ingredient prepared meals requires careful chain-of-custody and often full upstream certification.
- Strengthening Organic Enforcement (SOE) rules have increased requirements for handlers. Plan for Organic System Plans, inspections, and record-keeping from day one.

**Key principle**: You cannot put the USDA seal on a finished meal unless the entire handling chain supports it.

## India (NPOP + FSSAI)

- Use **NPOP** (National Programme for Organic Production) certified ingredients for credible “certified organic” claims.
- FSSAI regulates organic food labeling. Multi-ingredient products generally require a high percentage of certified organic content for strong claims.
- Maintain full traceability and segregation. Restaurants and cloud kitchens must avoid misleading claims.
- Partner with certified farmer groups or cooperatives. Individual small-farm certification is expensive; group certification is usually more practical.

## Universal Rules for Any Market

1. Never claim “100% organic” or “certified organic” unless you can prove the full chain.
2. Maintain physical segregation from conventional products.
3. Use only approved sanitizers and processing aids.
4. Keep lot-level records that can be audited.
5. Train every team member on organic integrity — contamination is easy and expensive.

## Practical Advice

Budget time and money for certification early. It is slower and more expensive than most founders expect. Treat the certification process as a core operational system, not a marketing checkbox.

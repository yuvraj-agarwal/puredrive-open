# Monetization Model — The "Red Hat" Approach

Giving away the operational IP does not mean giving away the business. The core foundation captures value through trust, network effects, and services while operators keep 100% of their store profits (no traditional royalties).

## 1. Brand & Trademark Licensing ("Certified Pure")

- Anyone can freely use the recipes, blueprints, SOPs, and financial models under their own local brand.
- To use the **PureDrive** name, logo, signage, and central app ecosystem, operators pay a low monthly verification fee (target: $299–$599 / location / month).
- Condition: Pass periodic third-party audits confirming 100% certified organic ingredients, seed-oil-free cooking, and operational standards.
- This creates a quality signal for customers while funding the core team.

## 2. Supply Chain Co-Op / Group Purchasing Organization (GPO)

- Aggregate demand across all independent locations for organic proteins, produce, oils, packaging, and supplies.
- Negotiate volume pricing that individual stores cannot achieve alone.
- Monetization: 3–5% administration fee embedded in the landed cost of goods distributed through preferred partners.
- Stronger network = better pricing for everyone = more attractive to new operators.

## 3. Managed SaaS & Hardware

While core software is open-source, most operators prefer managed solutions:

- **PureOS Cloud**: Hosted POS, kitchen display system (KDS), inventory with lot tracking, loyalty, and mobile ordering — $149–$249 / location / month.
- Hardware bundles: Pre-configured tablets, kitchen displays, printers, and optional drive-thru tech sold at a reasonable margin.
- Optional white-glove onboarding and training packages.

## 4. Optional Premium Services

- Certification support and audit facilitation
- Custom menu localization consulting
- Regional hub setup assistance
- Data insights and benchmarking across the network (anonymized)

## Why This Beats Traditional Franchising

Operators keep nearly all of their profits. Growth is permissionless and capital-light for the core. The foundation wins by becoming the trusted infrastructure layer rather than extracting royalties forever.

This is the Linux / Red Hat model applied to physical food service.
